Moving at your own pace, around Denver

A calm guide for older adults and the families helping them, with sources. Quotes from vetted movers are there if you want them.

Moving later in life is rarely about boxes. It is about choosing what comes along, saying goodbye to a place full of years, and finding a safe spot for the paperwork that matters. This guide is slow on purpose. It leaves out hurry-up advice and sticks to checkable facts, with the source named each time.

Where to begin

  1. Talk first, pack later

    Before anyone lifts a box, talk about what the person moving wants. What matters most in the new place? Which room, chair or garden is non-negotiable? These answers decide what you keep. A paced timeline, from twelve weeks out.

  2. Sort in short sessions

    One closet or one shelf per visit keeps the work from feeling endless. Sorting, donating and estate sales.

  3. Decide who helps

    Family, a senior move manager, a mover, or a mix. What senior move managers do, and what their trade association checks.

  4. Check the destination

    If the new home is an assisted living residence, a few official checks are worth doing before the lease is signed. Moving into senior living.

  5. Keep the important papers close

    Do not let the folder of documents ride in the truck. A short list of what to carry yourself.

Local help that costs nothing to ask for

The Denver Regional Council of Governments runs the federally designated Area Agency on Aging for the Denver region. Its pages describe a free online directory of community services called Network of Care, plus case management for people 60 and older. DRCOG lists 303-480-6700 as the number for its Aging and Disability Resource Center; confirm it on DRCOG’s Network of Care page before you call.

Common questions

What is a senior move manager?

A senior move manager is a professional who helps older adults and their families plan and carry out a move, including sorting, downsizing, coordinating movers and settling into the new home. NASMM, the trade association, says Senior Move Manager is a registered service mark that only its members may use.

Who is the Area Agency on Aging for the Denver area?

The Denver Regional Council of Governments (DRCOG) is the designated Area Agency on Aging and Aging and Disability Resource Center for its region, which its own pages list as Adams, Arapahoe, Broomfield, Clear Creek, Denver, Douglas, Gilpin and Jefferson counties.

Do I need a special mover to help my parent move?

No special license exists for it. A household-goods mover in Colorado needs a Public Utilities Commission permit, whoever the customer is. What helps is a crew with patience and a plan that matches your parent's pace.

Sources: NASMM FAQs; DRCOG Network of Care.
